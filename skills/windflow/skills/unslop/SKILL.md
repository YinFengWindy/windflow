# Unslop

Apply while drafting and editing. Preserve the author's meaning, intended tone, concrete facts, qualifications, and real uncertainty.

- State the point directly. Remove generic praise, canned openings, empty conclusions, and promises to help later.
- Replace vague claims with the actual mechanism, observation, source, or decision. Delete claims that carry no information.
- Prefer familiar words and active verbs. Use real project terms consistently rather than cycling through synonyms or inventing metaphors.
- Remove filler, forced contrasts, decorative emphasis, and repetitive sentence structures. Use lists only when the contents are naturally parallel or sequential.
- Keep complete, readable sentences. Concision is not a reason to drop the conditions that make a claim true.
- Match the user's language. Do not mechanically impose English punctuation or sentence-length rules on another language.
- Keep source attribution and labels for facts, inferences, and unknowns. Editing must not turn tentative evidence into certainty.

Use [technical-writing](../technical-writing/SKILL.md) to organize technical documents. This skill improves expression; it does not authorize new claims, scope, approvals, or edits to unrelated writing.
